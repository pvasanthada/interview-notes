# Behavioral Interview Framework

**Label:** `Interview Preparation`

## 1. Why Behavioral Matters More at Principal Level

At principal level, technical competence is assumed to be a baseline; behavioral interviews are frequently the deciding factor because the role is fundamentally about **influence without full authority** — across engineering, security, finance, and leadership.

## 2. STAR, Applied Well

**Situation → Task → Action → Result (+ Reflection)**

- **Situation (10%):** One or two sentences of context — enough to understand stakes, not a full history.
- **Task (10%):** What specifically you were responsible for.
- **Action (60%):** What *you* did — decisions, conversations, trade-offs, how you handled resistance. Use "I," not just "we."
- **Result (15%):** Quantified outcome where possible (time saved, incidents reduced, cost avoided, team unblocked).
- **Reflection (5%):** What you'd do differently, or what became a lasting standard/process.

## 3. Story Bank — Categories to Prepare

Have at least one strong story ready for each:

1. A time you **disagreed with a technical decision** and how you handled it.
2. A time you **influenced without direct authority**.
3. A **production incident** you led or contributed to resolving.
4. A time you had to **say no** to a stakeholder or simplify scope.
5. A time you **mentored** someone or grew a team's capability.
6. A time you made a **decision with incomplete information**.
7. A time a **decision you made turned out wrong**, and what you did next.
8. A time you had to **drive a standard/governance change** across teams that resisted it.

## 4. Handling Follow-Up Pressure

Principal interviews often probe your first answer with "why," "what if," or "what would you do differently" two or three times deep. Preparation tip: for each story, pre-think **one thing you'd change in hindsight** — interviewers read unprompted reflection as seniority, and a refusal to admit any imperfection as a red flag.

## 5. When STAR Doesn't Fit

For open-ended philosophy questions ("how do you approach technical debt," "how do you build trust with a new team"), don't force STAR. Instead:

1. State your point of view in one or two sentences.
2. Illustrate with a brief, concrete example.
3. Note a caveat or condition where your approach would change.

## 6. Red Flags to Avoid in Your Own Answers

- Blaming others without owning your part.
- Vague stories with no measurable result.
- Only "we" language, never "I."
- No acknowledgment of anything that went imperfectly.
- Stories that reveal poor judgment about risk, security, or people management.
